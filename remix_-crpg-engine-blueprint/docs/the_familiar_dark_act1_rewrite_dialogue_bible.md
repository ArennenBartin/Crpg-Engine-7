# The Familiar Dark — Act 1 Rewrite + Dialogue Bible

## Working Act Title

**Act 1: The Shuttered Hunger**

This document rewrites Act 1 into a fuller CRPG investigation with multiple suspects, stronger mystery structure, clearer player guidance, and dialogue for the known NPC cast.

It is designed to replace the current “talk to three witnesses → talk to Lazare → cave → verdict” checklist with a more theatrical, soap-heightened, Alderamontico-specific investigation.

The act still ends by opening the larger Nessa/Witness thread.

---

# 0. The Act’s Purpose

Act 1 is not only about clearing Lazare.

Act 1 teaches the player how Alderamontico thinks.

The town sees a body in the eastern caves. The body is partially Glass-taken, surrounded by mutilated animals, tally marks, and frantic writing. The town wants Lazare Behind the Shutters to be guilty because a vampire is an easy category.

But there is another obvious suspect: **Orin Vale**, the Glassworks Hand.

Orin is not obviously innocent. In fact, he is materially suspicious:
- he had access to Glassworks offcuts,
- he knew the victim Darro Keel,
- he lied about where he was,
- he bought candles and wrapping cloth before the incident,
- he is loudly gathering townspeople to accuse Lazare,
- and the cave logs eventually reveal that Orin gave Darro the shard that started his descent.

The truth is not “Lazare innocent, save him.”

The truth is more uncomfortable:

**Lazare did not kill Darro. Orin did not murder Darro. Darro became Grid-sick after using a Glassworks offcut in old rite-work, and the town’s fear turned the case into a monster story before the evidence could speak.**

Orin is guilty of negligence, concealment, cowardice, and desperate self-protection.

He is not guilty of murder.

That distinction matters because the full game later asks the player to make a harder distinction about Nessa:
she did something forbidden and catastrophic, but she is not the witch/murderer the Church needs her to be.

# 1. Core Mystery Triangle

## Victim

### Darro Keel

A local laborer and under-rite participant. He had connections to Mara Vey’s circle but was not one of the Witness-statue victims. He wanted contact, proof, or calm. Orin gave him a supposedly spent Glassworks offcut. Darro used it in the eastern caves with under-rite materials.

He became Grid-sick.

He killed animals under compulsion, not hunger. He wrote everything down while part of him still understood the horror.

By the time the player finds him, Darro is dead or converted enough that his remaining consciousness has become a cyberghost / Bound Remnant in the deep cave.

## Suspect 1: Lazare Behind the Shutters

The town’s preferred suspect.

He is a reclusive licensed vampire who never leaves his shuttered house. He is dangerous, old, and not harmless. He is the kind of person the town can imagine doing something terrible.

The evidence against him is mostly social:
- he is a vampire,
- animal mutilation looks like hunger,
- people are afraid of his house,
- he smelled the cave death before anyone told him,
- he refuses to explain how his mirrors saw the old road,
- he has a Church license, which makes people trust him less, not more.

He did not do it.

But he is not an innocent lamb.

## Suspect 2: Orin Vale, Glassworks Hand

The materially suspicious suspect.

He works the Glassworks edge. He knows shard heat, stress, furnace residue, and Glass-safe handling. He gave Darro a “spent” offcut. It was not spent enough.

He is guilt-ridden and terrified. To survive his own guilt, he becomes the loudest voice accusing Lazare.

He does not think he is lying. He thinks he is choosing the more likely monster.

He did not murder Darro.

But he helped create the conditions that destroyed him.

## Real Cause

Grid sickness from forceful/intimate exposure to active Glass during under-rite use.

The case solves when the player understands:
- animal remains were counted, not fed on,
- wounds are not feeding wounds,
- Glass stress starts inside the body,
- Darro’s logs show self-loss,
- Orin’s shard began the exposure chain,
- Lazare’s absence is real,
- Darro’s cyberghost is the final witness.

# 2. Tone Rules For Rewritten Dialogue

Every quest-facing dialogue node must do at least one of these:

1. Tell the player where to go.
2. Tell the player what was seen.
3. Tell the player who is suspected and why.
4. Add contradiction.
5. Reveal character bias.
6. Establish Alderamontico through concrete detail.

Do not let every NPC speak the thesis.

## Avoid

- “Category is not evidence” repeated everywhere.
- Everyone sounding like Aldric.
- Villagers explaining the Grid like scholars.
- Lazare sounding harmless.
- Orin sounding evil.
- Aldric sounding villainous.
- Nessa sounding purely innocent.
- Glass being treated as generic magic crystal.
- Poetic lines before the player knows the practical facts.

## Use

- purchases, schedules, gate marks, wounds, dust, keys, wax, soot, gloves, ledgers, shutters, roads, rope, tally marks, warm shards, wrong calm.
- villagers speaking locally and concretely.
- Church speakers being formal and procedural.
- Lazare speaking elegantly and bitterly.
- Orin speaking nervous, defensive, and performative in public.
- Aldric guiding clearly but interpreting rigidly.

# 3. Critical Path Summary

## Beat 0 — Opening Ceremony

The Pilgrim becomes an Intercessor. Aldric frames the first assignment: a cave death, a frightened town, a vampire suspect, and a Glassworks hand stirring the accusation.

Player chooses class discipline:
- Scholar
- Warrior
- Mystic

## Beat 1 — Office Briefing

Aldric lays out the case:
- Darro Keel is the victim.
- Lazare is the town’s named suspect.
- Orin Vale is the one loudly pressing the accusation.
- The body shows signs that do not fit feeding.
- The player must gather testimony before entering the cave.

The Case Board is introduced.

## Beat 2 — Public Accusation

The player sees Orin in the market or square gathering people against Lazare.

This makes Orin a real dramatic presence and gives the town a soap-theatrical public conflict.

## Beat 3 — Testimony Loop

The player gathers testimony from core witnesses:
- Dimos: purchases and town fear.
- Marta: body evidence.
- Orin: evasive Glassworks testimony.
- Holt: Lazare did not leave by the gate, but Orin did pass toward the old road.
- Bren: town fear and cordon atmosphere.
- optional Petra/Sela/Liss/Cosmas/Father Imre/Maro/Sister Vela/Riverman.

Each testimony points suspicion either toward Lazare or Orin, not directly to “the truth.”

## Beat 4 — Lazare Interview

Lazare remains frightening and suspicious:
- he knew the death before official word,
- he smelled or saw something through mirrors,
- he admits he is capable of hunger,
- he denies this killing clearly,
- he names Orin’s fear as useful.

The player should leave thinking:
“Lazare might not have done it, but I still do not trust him.”

## Beat 5 — Orin Pressure Point

After enough testimony, the player can confront Orin privately. He admits he knew Darro, denies causing the death, and tries to redirect suspicion back to Lazare.

If the player has Dimos/Holt/Petra clues, Orin becomes visibly unstable.

## Beat 6 — Forest Route

The player travels through:
- Old Processional Wood
- Glass-Touched Copse

These maps are not generic forests. They are old sacred roads, prayer trees, church markers, wrong roots, and Glass pressure leading toward the cave.

## Beat 7 — Cave Investigation

The cave contains:
- animal remains arranged by count,
- warm Glass shards,
- Darro’s logs,
- under-rite marks,
- Grid-sick enemies,
- and the cyberghost / Bound Remnant.

The logs gradually reveal:
- Orin gave Darro the shard,
- Darro used it repeatedly,
- Darro became Grid-sick,
- the animal deaths were compulsive counting/ordering,
- Darro was connected to Mara’s circle,
- and “It was never the statue that opened.”

## Beat 8 — Verdict

Aldric files the finding:
- Lazare is cleared.
- Darro died of Grid sickness.
- Orin supplied unsanctioned/unsafe Glass and concealed that fact.
- Orin is disciplined or marked for Church handling, but not charged with murder.
- The cave logs open the Nessa/Witness thread.

## Beat 9 — First Nessa Meeting

Warden Sefa permits the player to visit Nessa.

Nessa is behind bars, angry, exhausted, and difficult. She refuses the word “witch.” The player brings the cave log connection: Mara Vey.

Nessa points toward the Counted Cup cellar / under-town route.

## Beat 10 — Act End

The vampire case closes, but the larger case opens.

The final Act 1 text should make clear:
- the town was wrong loudly,
- the Church can file Lazare’s innocence,
- Orin’s guilt is containable,
- Nessa’s truth is not,
- and the cave dead wrote the first phrase of the real story.

# 5. Evidence Matrix

## Evidence That Points Toward Lazare

| Evidence | Source | Truth Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Darro’s body found among animal remains | opening report | looks like hunger but is not |
| “Pale figure” seen near old road | Liss/Cosmas rumor | mistaken or uncertain |
| Lazare’s house lies near residential route to cave | map geography | socially suspicious |
| Lazare smelled/sensed the death early | Lazare interview | true, but vampire senses are not guilt |
| People buy locks/candles because of Lazare | Dimos | proves fear, not murder |
| Church license implies dangerous hunger | High Clerk/Father Imre | true but not case-specific |

## Evidence That Points Toward Orin

| Evidence | Source | Truth Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orin bought seven candles, wrapping cloth, and oil | Dimos | used for off-record Glass handling / Darro meeting |
| Orin was seen on the old road | Holt/Cosmas | places him near the route |
| Glassworks dust found near cave shard | Orin/Petra clue | material link |
| Darro log says “O said it was spent” | Log 1 | direct link |
| Orin pushes Lazare’s guilt publicly | public scene | guilt displacement |
| Orin lies about knowing Darro | confrontation | concealment |

## Evidence That Reveals The Truth

| Evidence | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wounds are not feeding wounds | Marta | not Lazare’s hunger |
| Glass stress begins inside body | Orin/Petra/Marta | conversion/exposure |
| Animals are counted/arranged | forest/cave clues | compulsion, not feeding |
| Logs show Darro’s self-loss | cave | Grid sickness |
| Cyberghost speaks in counting fragments | boss | Darro remains |
| Mara named in final log | cave | Nessa thread opens |
| “It was never the statue that opened” | final log/end text | bigger Witness truth |

> He knew Darro.
>
> He handled shard offcuts.

>
> Marta at the lower graves.
>
> Holt at the gate.

# 12. Dimos / Provisioner Dialogue

## `dia_dimos`

### Baseline Before Assignment

> Looking for a lamp, a glove, or an opinion?
>
> First two cost coin.
>
> Last one costs more than people think.

### Investigation Opening

>
> Fine.
>
> I sell goods, not innocence. But people leave patterns on a counter whether they mean to or not.

Options:
1. “What did Lazare buy?”
2. “What did Orin buy?”
3. “What did Darro buy?”
4. “Record testimony.”

### Option: “What did Lazare buy?”

> Nothing.
>
> Lazare never buys candles. Never buys rope. Never buys salt. Never buys oil.
>
> People afraid of Lazare buy those things.
>

> Seven candles. Lamp oil. Wrapping cloth. Glass-safe twine.

> Said it was for furnace scrap.

### Option: “What did Darro buy?”

> Bread, twice. Cheap gloves once. A tin cup.

>
> Lazare bought nothing.

# 44. Final Design Rule

Act 1 should not feel like a checklist of witnesses.

It should feel like a town arguing in public over which kind of monster it can bear to name.

The player’s job is not to rescue Lazare.

The player’s job is to survive the difference between:
- fear,
- evidence,
- guilt,
- legal responsibility,
- spiritual contamination,
- and the truth a town is willing to admit.

That is the first Alderamontico lesson.
